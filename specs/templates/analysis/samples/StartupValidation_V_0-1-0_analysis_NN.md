# NN Analysis

## NN Analysis: Pre-Seed Strategic Assessment
A standalone review of the riskiest beliefs behind a regional meal-kit subscription
before committing seed capital. The model is descriptively plausible; the open
question is whether demand, retention, and unit economics hold outside the founding
city.

# NN Assumptions

## NN Assumptions: Urban professionals will pay a 35% premium over groceries for time saved.
## NN Assumptions: Monthly churn stays under 8% after the first three deliveries.
## NN Assumptions: Cold-chain logistics can be run profitably within a 60 km radius.
## NN Assumptions: Paid social acquires subscribers below a 90-day payback.

# NN Risks

## NN Risks: Incumbent grocers launch a competing kit and undercut on price.
## NN Risks: Churn spikes once introductory discounts end.
## NN Risks: Packaging and spoilage costs erode contribution margin.
## NN Risks: A single 3PL partner is a supply-chain single point of failure.

# NN SWOT

Strengths: chef-designed menus, strong founder brand in the launch city, flexible
weekly skip. Weaknesses: thin margins, capital-intensive cold chain, no proprietary
tech. Opportunities: corporate wellness bundles, private-label retail, adjacent
cities on the same logistics lane. Threats: grocery incumbents, ingredient
inflation, subscription fatigue.

# NN Keys

## NN Keys: Retention past delivery three
The whole model hinges on getting subscribers past the trial window into habitual
use; every experiment should ladder up to this.
## NN Keys: Route density
Contribution margin is a function of deliveries per km driven — geographic
concentration matters more than raw subscriber count.

# NN Coherence

## NN Coherence: Premium pricing vs. mass-market acquisition
The 35% premium assumption and the paid-social payback assumption pull against each
other — a premium product usually needs a lower-CAC, trust-led channel.
## NN Coherence: 60 km radius vs. adjacent-city opportunity
The logistics assumption and the expansion opportunity are consistent only if new
cities sit on the same cold-chain lane.

# NN Experiments

## NN Experiments: Four-week concierge pilot in two neighbourhoods
Hand-pack and deliver 50 subscriptions manually to measure real churn and willingness
to pay before investing in a facility.
## NN Experiments: Price-ladder landing-page test
Run three price points against identical creative to find the premium the market
actually tolerates.
## NN Experiments: Single-route density simulation
Model delivery cost per order at 20, 40, and 80 stops on one route to size the
route-density effect.
